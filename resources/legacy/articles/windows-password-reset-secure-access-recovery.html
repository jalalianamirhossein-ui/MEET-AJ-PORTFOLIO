Recover Windows Access with an Authorized Password Reset

Separate local, AD and Entra recovery, assess EFS/dependencies, reset with PowerShell and diagnose recurring lockouts.

Microsoft

Meet AJ technical article

Start reading

Identify the account before resetting

Local, AD DS, Microsoft Account and Entra identities have different recovery paths. Resetting the wrong account risks services/tasks and local EFS or stored credentials. Confirm identity and dependencies before using the account’s recovery method.

Reset, password change and unlock

An administrator reset differs from a user changing their own password. Unlock clears failed-login lockout without changing the password. BitLocker keys and Windows Hello PINs are separate credentials.

Example: reset a staff-OU account

Helpdesk has reset delegation on the staff OU. Ops.user represents a human account. Do not apply this general workflow to service accounts or Domain Admins. Match request, identity and OU before execution.

Permissions and account-specific modules

Examples target Windows 11 or Server 2022/2025 with x64 Windows PowerShell 5.1. AD commands require RSAT ActiveDirectory.

Use the target device for local accounts and an approved workstation for AD. VM capacity is irrelevant here. Prepare DC connectivity and recovery-console access.

Local changes need LocalAccounts and elevation. AD changes need reset rights on the target OU. Verify requester and target identity through Helpdesk procedures.

LAPS and cloud identities

Manage LAPS accounts through its retrieval/rotation workflow. Set-ADAccountPassword applies only to AD DS. Microsoft Account/Entra use their official recovery paths. Password-reset disks apply to local accounts and must exist beforehand.

EFS, DPAPI and service accounts

Keep secrets out of transcripts/tickets. Assess local EFS, private-key and DPAPI loss risks before reset. Service accounts need dependency-coordinated rotation. Release BitLocker keys only after matching device/key ID.

Reset an AD or local account with PowerShell

Inspect the human AD account from an approved RSAT workstation. Ops.user is a placeholder. Match its account and OU to the request before resetting. 

Import-Module ActiveDirectory -ErrorAction Stop
$AccountName = 'ops.user'
Get-ADUser -Identity $AccountName -Properties Enabled,LockedOut,PasswordLastSet,DistinguishedName |
    Select-Object SamAccountName,Enabled,LockedOut,PasswordLastSet,DistinguishedName
$NewPassword = Read-Host 'Enter approved temporary password' -AsSecureString
Set-ADAccountPassword -Identity $AccountName -Reset -NewPassword $NewPassword -ErrorAction Stop
Set-ADUser -Identity $AccountName -ChangePasswordAtLogon $true -ErrorAction Stop
Get-ADUser -Identity $AccountName -Properties PasswordLastSet | Select-Object SamAccountName,PasswordLastSet

A successful cmdlet usually has no output. Forced password change can make PasswordLastSet zero or special-valued. The user must log on from a domain workstation with DC connectivity and change the password. Unlock only after confirming lockout and resolving stale credentials.

For a human local account, use this separate elevated PowerShell procedure on the target device. 

$LocalAccountName = 'local.ops'
Get-LocalUser -Name $LocalAccountName -ErrorAction Stop
$LocalPassword = Read-Host 'New local password' -AsSecureString
Set-LocalUser -Name $LocalAccountName -Password $LocalPassword -ErrorAction Stop

local.ops is a placeholder. Recover/rotate Windows LAPS-managed accounts through the organizational LAPS workflow so manual changes do not conflict with its lifecycle.

Recurring lockout or failed login

Access denied calls for delegation/elevation checks. Password-policy failures require complexity, history and fine-grained-policy review. Repeated lockouts often come from stale task/service credentials. For unreachable DCs, inspect DNS and secure channels. Repeated resets do not repair the cause.

Audit resets and lockouts

Appropriate audit policy records 4724 reset attempts and 4740 lockouts. Inspect the system where the event occurred and forward to SIEM. Missing events might reflect policy rather than absence of change.

Secure secret delivery and independent keys

Do not extract or roll back the prior password. Set another new secret through the authorized workflow when needed and deliver it securely. Protect EFS recovery certificates, BitLocker keys and emergency identities before incidents. AD system-state restoration is not a user-reset method.

Test login after the change

Record time, operator, account and request ID, never passwords. The AD user needs DC connectivity for login and mandatory password change. Unlock after correcting stale credentials.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a password reset replace a BitLocker key?

No. BitLocker recovery keys are independent.

Can the previous password be rolled back?

No. Set another new credential through the authorized workflow if necessary.
